# Working with breeders and physicians

## Start from the decision

Ask once, before analysis: what will you decide with this, by when, with what data, and what would count as success? Write the answers in two lines at the top of the project README. If the decision cannot change with the result, say so and stop.

## Deliverables

* Breeders: a ranked table (ID, predicted value, accuracy or reliability), expected gain, effect on diversity and inbreeding, suggested crosses or matings, and the environments the prediction holds for. Provide a CSV with stable IDs, ready for the breeding database.
* Physicians: gene, HGVS with build and transcript, classification with criteria, phenotype fit, limits (regions not covered, what a negative result does not exclude), follow-up options, and a reanalysis date. Keep it to one page. Never state a diagnosis; state the evidence.

## Plain language

* Lead with the answer, then the uncertainty as an interval or a frequency ("about 7 of 10 selected lines will beat the check"), then the caveat that most changes the action.
* Use absolute numbers beside relative ones, define each technical term once, and avoid abbreviations in the summary.
* One figure per message. Use `plotting`.

## Meetings

Send the one-page summary first. End with decisions, owners, and dates. Record disagreements and the data that would settle them.

## Do not

Overstate certainty, hide a failed validation, or send raw tables when a decision table will do.
