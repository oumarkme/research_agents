# AI-assisted writing and disclosure policies (publishers, funders, institutions), biomedical/genetics/genomics

Access date for all items: 2026-11-05. Most pages show no version number; "page version" is recorded as what the page itself displayed. Items marked [SECONDARY] were not read on the primary page (paywall/403/redirect) and must be re-checked before being cited in a manuscript.

## 1. Publisher and body rules: AI as author, disclosure, location, "language editing" exemption

### Takeaway
Every body surveyed bars AI authorship and makes the human authors accountable. Disclosure rules split into two camps: ICMJE, COPE, WAME, JAMA, Frontiers, Bioinformatics (OUP/ISCB) have no stated carve-out for editing, whereas Elsevier, Wiley, PLOS, Springer Nature (with an internal conflict), and MDPI [SECONDARY] exempt basic spelling/grammar/language editing. Generative rewriting of prose is not covered by the exemptions that were found in primary text.

### Cited Findings
- ICMJE (page "Use of AI by Authors", no date/version shown, footer "(c) 2026 ICMJE"): journals should "require authors to disclose at submission whether they used AI-assisted technologies"; disclosure "in both the cover letter and the submitted work in the appropriate section if applicable"; "Chatbots (such as ChatGPT) and other AI-assisted tools should not be listed as authors"; "Nondisclosure of AI use may require corrective action and may be construed as misconduct in some circumstances". No editing exemption was found on this page (the fetch tool could not confirm sections II.A.4 and IV.A.3.d) — [ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html)
- COPE position statement "Authorship and AI tools" (last reviewed 13 Feb 2023 per page; version 1 dated 20 Aug 2024): "AI tools cannot be listed as an author of a paper"; use in writing, images, or data analysis must be disclosed in Materials and Methods or a similar section, naming the tool and how it was used; "Authors are fully responsible for the content of their manuscript, even those parts produced by an AI tool" — [COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/authorship-and-ai-tools)
- WAME (revised 31 May 2023; original 20 Jan 2023; possibly outdated): "Chatbots cannot be authors"; "Authors should be transparent when chatbots are used and provide information about how they were used"; also report prompts used to generate new text; "Editors and peer reviewers should specify, to authors and each other, any use of chatbots" — [WAME](https://wame.org/page3.php?id=106)
- Elsevier (page shows "Policy updated: July 2026"; also governs Cell Press titles, but the AJHG/Cell author page returned 403 and was not read): authors must "disclose the use of AI Tools for manuscript preparation in a separate AI declaration statement" placed immediately above the references under "Declaration of Generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process"; "Basic checks of grammar, spelling and punctuation need no declaration"; spelling/grammar tools "can be used by authors without disclosure" — [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier)
- Wiley (page shows no date): "Authors must also disclose their use of AI Technologies when submitting a manuscript to a Wiley journal"; substantial editing/development/translation must be "described transparently and integrated into the manuscript"; "AI Tools used solely for spelling, grammar, and general editing are not included in these disclosure requirements"; editors may request disclosure during review or after publication — [Wiley](https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html)
- PLOS (page shows no date): "We do not require disclosure of AI use for language improvements"; disclosure of other use must be "in the body of the article" (Methods subsection, Acknowledgements, or figure legend) with the tool, how it was used, and how outputs were checked; translation use recommended to disclose; code generation or analysis scripts require disclosure; cover letters need no disclosure — [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/ethical-publishing-practice)
- Springer Nature group (no date; footer 2026): AI cannot be an author; "The use of generative AI tools should be declared in the Introduction or Acknowledgements of the manuscript"; LLM use "must be documented in the manuscript's Methods section (or equivalent)"; FAQ: "The use of an AI tool for 'AI assisted copy editing' purposes does not need to be declared"; a later FAQ line says use "should be declared in line with our AI guidance". The page is internally inconsistent, so treat the copy-editing exemption as uncertain and check the target journal — [Springer Nature](https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/ai/ai-guidance-for-our-researchers-and-communities)
- Nature Portfolio: the primary page (nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai) redirected to a cookie gate and was not read. [SECONDARY] summaries say LLMs cannot be authors, LLM use beyond "AI-assisted copy editing" is documented in Methods, copy-editing improvements need no declaration, and generative images are barred except when part of the research — [search summary of third-party pages](https://www.enago.cn/responsible-ai-movement/publisher-ai-guidelines/nature-ai-guidelines); one source disagreed (all AI use declared) — unresolved
- Science/AAAS: primary page returned 403. [SECONDARY] news and blog summaries: January 2023 ban reversed in November 2023 to disclosure-based allowance; details in Methods, including tool, version, and prompt; AI cannot be an author; AI not allowed for reviewers — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/729880), [Manusights, updated 2 Apr 2026](https://manusights.com/blog/science-ai-policy)
- JAMA Network (instructions for authors, ~250k-character page, only partly read): "Authors should report the use of artificial intelligence, language models, machine learning, or similar technologies"; authors "must take responsibility"; AI to draft Viewpoints, Letters, and A Piece of My Mind "is not permitted"; "Reviewers are instructed to not submit confidential manuscripts, abstracts, or other text into a chatbot". The dedicated "Use of AI in Publication and Research" section was not reached; a 2026 consensus paper reportedly says JAMA and BMJ require model name, version, manufacturer, and the nature of generated content (unverified) — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/pages/instructions-for-authors)
- Bioinformatics (OUP; follows ISCB LLM policy): LLM use "should be disclosed both in cover letters to editors and in the Methods or Acknowledgements section"; allowed "as an aid to correct written text (spell checkers, grammar checkers)" and translation (author responsible); "It is not acceptable to use LLMs or related technologies to draft papers" from a prompt — [Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/pages/general_instructions)
- Frontiers: "If AI tools have been used to generate main text, then this must be clearly disclosed in the acknowledgments"; AI-generated or AI-edited content acknowledged (acknowledgments and, where applicable, Methods) with tool name, version, model, source; AI cannot be an author; prompts and outputs encouraged as supplementary files. Note "AI-edited" is within scope, so no language-editing carve-out was seen — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/guidelines/policies-and-publication-ethics)
- MDPI [SECONDARY, primary 403]: AI not an author; substantive use disclosed in Acknowledgments (Methods for some journals such as IJMS); basic language editing exempt — [CASRAI guide](https://casrai.org/guides/mdpi-generative-ai-authorship-policy), [Manusights IJMS](https://manusights.com/blog/international-journal-of-molecular-sciences-ai-policy)

### Inferences
- Safe default across all publishers: write a one-sentence declaration whenever an LLM rewrote, restructured, or generated sentences, even if only for "polish". Declaration costs little; undisclosed use is the sanctioned behavior (ICMJE: possible misconduct).
- The exemptions (Elsevier, Wiley, PLOS) are worded as spelling, grammar, and general editing/language improvement. An LLM that rewrites for style sits at the edge; the wording favors disclosure when the model changes sentence structure or content, and favors no disclosure when it only flags or fixes errors. This line is my reading, not stated by any publisher.
- Disclosure location varies: Methods (COPE, Springer, Science, Bioinformatics), Acknowledgements (Frontiers, MDPI, Springer alt.), dedicated statement above references (Elsevier), cover letter plus text (ICMJE, Bioinformatics). Follow the target journal.

### Gaps
- Primary text not read: Nature Portfolio, Science, MDPI, Cell Press/AJHG (403/redirect), BMJ (page fetched had no AI text), NAR, Genetics in Medicine/ACMG (search returned nothing), Lancet family, JAMA dedicated AI section.
- No publisher page showed a version number; dates are only where stated above.

## 2. Funder and institutional policy (NIH, Taiwan NSTC, NTU/NTUH)

### Takeaway
NIH bars AI use in peer review and treats applications "substantially developed by AI" as non-original. Taiwan has no NSTC research-writing guideline that I could find; NTU issued a specific "generative AI use and research integrity" guideline in June 2026 that requires disclosure with tool, version, date, purpose, and degree of involvement, and warns that heavy undisclosed use may count as plagiarism.

### Cited Findings
- NIH NOT-OD-25-132 (released 17 July 2025; effective for the 25 Sept 2025 receipt date): NIH "will not consider applications that are either substantially developed by AI"; post-award detection may lead to misconduct referral; six-application-per-PI-per-year limit; "NIH will continue to employ the latest technology in detection of AI-generated content" — [NIH](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-25-132.html)
- NIH NOT-OD-23-149 (23 June 2023): NIH "prohibits NIH scientific peer reviewers from using natural language processors, large language models"; uploading application material to online AI tools "violates the NIH peer review confidentiality and integrity requirements" — [NIH](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-23-149.html)
- NTU 《國立臺灣大學生成式 AI 使用與研究誠信指引》, dated 115 年 6 月 (June 2026), read from the PDF: 「本校原則上不禁止將生成式 AI 作為學習或研究之輔助工具」; disclosure item: 「應於論文、研究報告、附錄、方法說明、致謝或依期刊要求，揭露所使用工具之名稱、版本、日期、使用目的、操作方式、介入程度」; keep logs of important prompts, inputs, outputs, and human revisions; verify citations and data; do not enter personal data, unpublished data, or review documents into open tools; reviewers 「原則上不得將受審文件輸入開放式生成式 AI 工具」; example misconduct: heavy, influential, undisclosed AI use 「情節重大者，可能構成…抄襲」; AI rewriting or translating one's own published work without citation or disclosure may be self-plagiarism; units may add stricter rules — [NTU ORI PDF](https://ori.ntu.edu.tw/ethics/download/fileSn/25)
- Taiwan executive branch: the 2023 NSTC-drafted 「行政院及所屬機關使用生成式AI參考指引」 targets public-sector work, not research writing; public universities may apply it by analogy [SECONDARY news] — [Technews](https://technews.tw/2023/07/19/nstc-releases-draft-reference-guidelines-for-generative-ai/)
- April 2026 news: NSTC told the Legislative Yuan that project reviewers must not feed review content to generative AI tools; Academia Sinica published its own AI guideline and has internal AI-detection software [SECONDARY news, not verified on a primary page] — [Chinatimes](https://wantrich.chinatimes.com/news/20260430900107-420501)

### Inferences
- NTUH-specific guidance was not found; as an NTUH postdoc, the NTU guideline and unit rules are the likely applicable institutional text (affiliation to be confirmed with the research integrity office).
- NTU's expectation to log prompts and outputs is stricter than most journals and is easy to meet with a short analysis-log entry.

### Gaps
- No NSTC (國科會) page specific to AI in research writing or grant applications was found; no NTUH page found. NTU document is the June 2026 version only; superseding versions not checked.

## 3. Reviewer-side rules

### Takeaway
All sources agree: do not upload manuscripts or review text to public AI tools. A few allow secure or approved tools, and a few allow polishing one's own review wording with disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- Elsevier: "Reviewers should not upload the material/manuscript or any part thereof into a generative AI tool"; no AI help with assessing the manuscript — [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier)
- Wiley: reviewers who use AI to improve wording must disclose to the handling editor; uploading manuscripts is not permitted; editors may disregard reports showing inappropriate AI use — [Wiley](https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html)
- PLOS: "AI tools cannot serve as peer reviewers"; copyediting one's own comments allowed if no confidential content is included — [PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/ethical-publishing-practice)
- Springer Nature: "Delegating peer review to an LLM" is not permitted; reviewers "should not upload manuscript content to unsecured or public AI systems" but may use secure or institutionally approved tools — [Springer Nature](https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/ai/ai-guidance-for-our-researchers-and-communities)
- Frontiers: no upload to external tools; use of external tools needs prior discussion with editors and disclosure to authors — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/guidelines/policies-and-publication-ethics)
- Bioinformatics: "It is not acceptable to submit manuscripts received for review to an LLM" — [Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/pages/general_instructions)
- WAME, NIH, JAMA, NTU: see sections 1-2.

### Inferences
- Local, offline models are not explicitly addressed by most policies except Springer Nature ("secure or institutionally-approved"); ask the editor before using one.

### Gaps
- Nature, Science, Cell Press, BMJ reviewer text not read first-hand.

## 4. Detection, screening, and consequences

### Takeaway
Enforcement is mostly reactive: readers or sleuths find chatbot residue or fake references, and papers are retracted for nondisclosure. NIH states it uses AI-detection technology. Large-scale studies show LLM-style vocabulary is detectable in aggregate but not proof for individual papers.

### Cited Findings
- Physica Scripta (IOP Publishing), 2023: a reader (Guillaume Cabanac) spotted the phrase "Regenerate response" in the paper; authors admitted ChatGPT use; retracted; IOP called it "a breach of our ethical policies". Details from aggregator coverage, original Nature report and Retraction Watch page not read — [Asiae summary](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2023091209440262767)
- Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology, February 2024: paper retracted over AI-generated figures [SECONDARY] — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2024-02-ai-generated-disproportioned-rat-genitalia.pdf)
- Neurosurgical Review, 2025: commentaries and letters retracted for strong indications of undisclosed LLM text [SECONDARY, search summary]; Discover Artificial Intelligence (Springer Nature) retraction for undeclared generative AI [SECONDARY blog] — [journalmetrics.org](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/undeclared-ai-use-retraction-springer-nature-2026)
- Kobak et al., "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary" (arXiv 2406.07016; Science Advances 2025, doi 10.1126/sciadv.adt3813): excess style words ("delves", "showcasing", "crucial", "pivotal") in 2024 PubMed abstracts imply at least ~10-13.5% processed with LLMs, higher in some subcorpora; estimates changed across versions; lower bounds — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v5)
- NIH: detection technology stated (see section 2). Academia Sinica: internal detection software [SECONDARY].
- WAME: editors need detection tools — [WAME](https://wame.org/page3.php?id=106)

### Inferences
- Literal "Certainly, here is" retractions: I did not find a verified case; the verified type is "Regenerate response" plus fake or hallucinated references. Do not cite the exact phrase case without confirming on Retraction Watch.
- Because aggregate vocabulary shifts are measurable, frequent marker words are a screening risk independent of any detector; the safest practice is accurate disclosure plus human verification of every reference and claim.

### Gaps
- No journal-published description of routine detector use found; detector accuracy and false-positive evidence not gathered here.
- Retraction Watch primary pages not retrieved.

## 5. Polishing versus AI-generated content, and wording examples

### Takeaway
Policies draw the line between tools that fix surface errors (spelling, grammar, readability) and tools that generate or restructure content (drafting, summarizing, translating, code, figures). Only the former is exempt, and only at some publishers.

### Cited Findings
- Exempt-type language: Elsevier ("Basic checks of grammar, spelling and punctuation"), Wiley ("solely for spelling, grammar, and general editing"), PLOS ("language improvements"), Springer Nature FAQ ("AI assisted copy editing") — see section 1 links.
- Not exempt: Wiley substantial editing or translation, PLOS code generation and recommended translation disclosure, Bioinformatics drafting from prompts is "not acceptable", JAMA bars drafting opinion pieces, Frontiers "AI-edited" content.
- Elsevier declaration should state which tool, why, and how closely the author oversaw it — [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier)
- Example wording (my draft, built from the elements journals request, not quoted from any journal): "During manuscript preparation, the authors used [tool name, version, date] to improve the language and readability of the text. The authors reviewed and edited all output and take full responsibility for the content." Add scope ("sentence-level rewording of the Discussion; no content or references generated") and say whether prompts are available on request.

### Inferences
- For a postdoc wanting natural-sounding text: use the model for specific edits, keep your own sentences as the base, verify every reference and number, keep a log (matches NTU item 4), and include the declaration where the target journal asks. A natural-reading result and a truthful disclosure are compatible; hiding the use is the risk.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed analysis of how editors operationalize "language editing" was retrieved (Peer Review Congress abstract and a 2026 consensus paper surfaced in search but were not read).
